# An eye can be trained. Chemistry that didn't exist yet can't.

Titanium white wasn't a commercial pigment before 1916. A "seventeenth-century"
painting containing it isn't a matter of opinion — it's mass spectrometry.

---

## Anachronistic materials

The Vermeer forgeries that timed themselves out — caught by their own
materials, decades after they fooled every eye that looked at them.

---

## What gets dated, and how

- paper and canvas stock
- pigment composition
- the radiocarbon problem for anything organic
- what a forger can, and can't, fake about their own materials

---

## "Looks right" and "tests right" are different claims

Only one of them survives a lab. The most damning evidence against a forgery
is usually the most boring — not a clumsy brushstroke, a lab result.
